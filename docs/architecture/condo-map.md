# Admin condo map

`/admin/condo-map` shows the developments from the floor-plan masters on a Mapbox map, each on its own site: the
whole building as its detailed 3D models (grey massing where they don't load), a chosen floor cut open with its units
painted on it by bedroom count, and a chosen unit's card with its plan. It is admin-only and unlisted (not in the admin menu) while J
decides what it becomes. J's choices, 24 Oct 2026: admins only; all nine developments. 25 Oct 2026: 32 Gilstead
added, and 12 On Shan taken off (it is a single-owner serviced-apartment building now, Weave Suites – Novena, so its
units aren't sold one by one; its package is kept in the masters), so nine. Each new development in the masters joins
the map with a re-export. 28 Oct 2026: every development has detailed 3D models, as 33 Residences had from the start
(J: "can we do it for the rest as well"); until then the other eight were grey massing.

## Decisions

- **Mapbox GL JS from its CDN, not a dependency.** The map is an admin preview, and its library is 1.9 MB.
  `app/admin/condo-map/useMapboxGl.ts` loads `mapbox-gl@3.31.0` from jsDelivr only on this page, with each file
  pinned to its version and checked by its SRI hash, so a changed file doesn't run. The types it uses are local
  (`mapbox-gl-types.ts`). A new version means new hashes (and a look at the map).
- **A public token only, read on the server.** `MAPBOX_PUBLIC_TOKEN` must be a `pk.` token (anything else counts as
  none, so a secret `sk.` token can't reach a browser); restrict it in Mapbox to the app's URLs. Without one the page
  still lists the developments and units and says what the map needs.
- **The data stays behind the admin check.** The developments' files live in `catalog/condo-map` (generated by the
  masters' `_tools/mapexport/export_condo_map.py`; see its README) and reach the browser only through
  `/api/admin/condo-map/[...path]`, which checks admin access first and serves only `<slug>/map.json`,
  `<slug>/plans/<picture>`, `<slug>/overlays/<plan>.webp` and `<slug>/models/<model>.glb`
  (`lib/condo-map/condo-map-paths.ts`). `next.config.ts`
  traces the folder into that route and the index into the page. The data is checked as it is read
  (`lib/condo-map/condo-map-data.ts`), so a changed export fails loudly.
- **The developments' models are in Git LFS** (`*.glb`), like the catalogue's models. The masters build them from
  each unit's brochure plan (walls, windows and balcony doors read off the plan, laid on the unit's outline) and the
  brochure's pictures (colours, roofs, fins, screens): `_tools/models3d` there. All nine come to about 17 MB, and the
  map loads every development's models when it opens.
- **What a view shows is pure and tested.** `condo-map-view.ts` (choosing a development, floor or unit; a unit brings
  its floor) and `condo-map-layers.ts` (the sources, layers, filters and colours) don't touch Mapbox;
  `scripts/test-condo-map.ts` runs the filters against the real features. `condo-map-mapbox.ts` does the Mapbox work
  and the hooks call it from their effects.
- **A unit is on every storey it takes.** A duplex or a penthouse with its attic has a part on each of its storeys, so
  it is painted and can be picked on each; choosing it keeps the floor already chosen when that is one of its storeys,
  and its card names them ("1st–2nd storey"). An area with an attic void says so ("+ attic void"), and its square
  feet then include the void.
- **The page says what didn't work.** A refused token, a map style that didn't load, a load that takes over 20 s, a
  development whose data didn't load (named in the list) and a detailed development whose models didn't load (drawn as
  massing instead, with a note) each show as such. The data route answers a Git LFS pointer (models not pulled) with a
  404, so Mapbox never gets a pointer as a model, and refuses folder names that are object properties
  (`constructor`, `__proto__`).
- **Mapbox's old buildings are kept off the sites** by a clip layer on each footprint 2.5 m in, and, where a browser
  draws one anyway (Safari did in the prototype), by marking it "selected", which the map's config draws clear
  (`condo-map-basemap.ts`). A second clip layer, labels only, on the development's whole lot with its footprint
  (`labelClip`, 28 Oct 2026), takes away Mapbox's point-of-interest labels on the site; it touches nothing next door.
- **House numbers go when you're close in** (29 Oct 2026). Mapbox's house numbers stood on the models ("21" on
  21 Richards, "32" on 32 Gilstead). The Standard style draws them with its place labels, after every layer a page
  adds, so no clip layer can take them away (the label clip above was meant to, and didn't). They show from zoom 17,
  so the map turns Standard's place labels off from zoom 16.5 in (`hidePlaceLabelsWhenClose`) and on again further
  out, where area names help. Street and point-of-interest names stay at every zoom.
- **A chosen floor reads at a glance** (J, 28 Oct 2026). Its units are painted by bedroom count in the masters'
  viewers' unit-chart hues (their dark theme's, which hold up in the map's daylight; a studio or an unknown count
  stays cream), the chosen unit orange, with a key under the floor's units in the panel. The storeys above stay as a
  5 % white ghost, enough to see the building's outline (it was 16 %, which hid the rooms).
- **A chosen unit's plan lies on its floor** (J, 30 Oct 2026: buyers "can see where the layout is relative to the
  building"). The masters fit each unit's brochure plan onto its outline (`_tools/models3d/plans_on_ground.py`), and
  the export draws it as a WebP (`<slug>/overlays/`) with its four corners and its floor's height (`plans` in
  map.json). The map shows the chosen unit's plan only, as a Mapbox image source raised 6 cm over its floor's paint
  (`raster-elevation`) and lit by itself; the rest of the floor stays painted by bedrooms, so the floor still reads
  at a glance. Mapbox draws a raised picture after the 3D layers and depth-tests it, so the chosen unit's orange box
  and the ghost of the storeys above would hide it: both go while a plan shows, and the orange edge is drawn into the
  picture instead (J's choice). The picture's source and layer are made when the first plan shows and pointed at the
  next one after (`showCondoPlan`).
- **Only a plan that lies the right way is shown** (J, 30 Oct 2026). A unit with a nearly symmetric outline fits its
  plan turned or mirrored almost as well, and a wrong one would put the balcony on the wrong side. The export fits each
  plan again from all eight turns and mirrors and shows it only when its own fits clearly best (the masters'
  `_tools/mapexport/plan_overlays.py`): 279 of the 426 units. Where the outline can't tell, the windows can (J,
  30 Oct 2026, "proceed"): each orientation is laid on the building, and the right one puts the plan's glass on the
  building's outside and its outer walls on the outside (`plan_windows.py`). Checked on the units the outline
  settles, it never chose otherwise at the thresholds used; it adds 71 units, 350 of the 426, 9 of them in another
  orientation than the masters' fit recorded (their 3D models still read the facade the recorded way). The rest are
  mostly plans mirrored along their facade, which puts the windows on the same walls either way. A unit without a plan
  on the map keeps its orange box, and its card says its plan isn't laid on the floor yet. A mirrored unit's plan is
  mirrored, with its words turned back to read forwards. An attic's plan isn't shown: the map doesn't offer an attic
  as a floor.
- **A chosen unit is kept in view.** A development's framing (tilted 50 degrees, turned 35) could leave a unit at the
  map's edge (32 Gilstead's #03-02, cut off). Once the camera settles, a unit whose outline at its storey isn't inside
  the map with a margin is brought to the middle, the zoom, tilt and turn kept (`keepUnitInView`); a move the user
  makes is left alone.
- **Credits.** Each development's credits (the brochures; SLA's land lots under the Singapore Open Data Licence;
  OneMap) are shown under the map, as the licences ask. No tiles or satellite pictures are stored.
